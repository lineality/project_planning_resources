# Project Planning Resources
At a high, overview, level...

### 1. Needs & Goals
Make sure you have a clear idea (as clear a perception as possible) of what project is trying to do. The process and details will vary depending on who the project is for (yourself, customer, other team, user, stakeholder, regulator, etc.). Here is a rubric for mapping out the problem-space for assessing Needs and Goals in many common cases:
- https://github.com/lineality/needs_goals_assessment_disambiguation 

### 2. Project Areas
Projects can be surprisingly different from each other in the details, but nearly all projects share the same overall 'Project Area' topics where you should inquire and plan. (Failing to understand these common areas is also where most problems and issues arise; better planning can prevent those issues.) 
- https://github.com/lineality/project_areas_for_project_and_product_management 

### 3. Definitions & Systems: Maintainable & Sustainable, Healthy Hygienic Workflow
As a more advanced area of planning (and process during the project) understanding the details and dynamics of what kind of systems you are working with can also be very helpful. This includes making sure your goals are soundly constructed.
- https://github.com/lineality/definition_behavior_studies 


## Specific Areas

For specific types of projects, such as Production-Data-Science or Software Development...


#### Production Data Science Planning
Data-Science projects increasingly need to consider 'production' project factors as purely academic analysis gets applied in real world situations.
- https://github.com/lineality/production_datascience_design_areas 


#### Mode & Case-Handling
Most software and scripting projects should consider how "Modes" and "Cases" are handled. This can be an important set of criteria that may factor into choices such as what language and tech-stack to use.
- https://github.com/lineality/modes_and_case_handling/blob/main/mode_case_handling_framework_summary.md
